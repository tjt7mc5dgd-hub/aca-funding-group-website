Funding solutions built on the purchase of future receivables

Each solution below is a purchase and sale of a specified amount of your future receivables — not a loan, and not an extension of credit. What differs is the use case, the remittance structure, and what underwriting needs to see.

How to read these terms. Purchase price is the amount ACA Funding Group pays your business up front. Specified amount (also called the purchased amount) is the total dollar value of future receivables sold. Specified percentage is the agreed share of receivables from which remittance is drawn. Because this is a purchase and not a loan, there is no interest rate, no APR, and no fixed maturity date — the structure is expressed in dollars and percentages, disclosed in the receivables purchase agreement.

Solution 01

Merchant cash advance

A merchant cash advance is the purchase of a fixed dollar amount of your business's future receivables at a discount. ACA Funding Group pays the purchase price up front; your business delivers the specified amount over time out of receivables as they are collected.

Who it fits: operating businesses with steady card settlements or deposit volume that need capital faster than a bank timeline allows, including owners whose personal credit would fail a conventional underwriting screen.

Typical structure

Purchase price
Paid to the business up front by ACH or wire, typically after bank verification.
Receivables purchased
A specified dollar amount of future receivables, greater than the purchase price. The difference is the discount, not interest.
Specified percentage
An agreed share of daily receivables from which remittance is drawn.
Remittance
Daily (business days) or weekly ACH debit from the business operating account.
Duration
No fixed maturity date. Delivery of the specified amount ends when it has been remitted in full.
Reconciliation
Available where a fixed estimated debit is used, so collections track actual receivables.

Required documents

  • Completed ACA Funding Group application
  • Last 3–4 months of complete business bank statements (all pages)
  • Government-issued photo ID for the signing owner
  • Voided business check or bank letter for the operating account
  • Merchant processing statements, where card volume is material
  • Entity formation document or EIN letter, if requested

Solution 02

Working capital advances

The same receivables purchase structure, sized and timed around a specific operating need rather than general cash flow — inventory buys, payroll coverage across a slow cycle, equipment deposits, a build-out, or mobilizing on a newly awarded contract.

Who it fits: businesses with a defined, near-term use of funds where the resulting revenue or cost savings can be described — distributors, contractors, restaurants, and service businesses with clear seasonality of expense.

Typical structure

Purchase price
Sized to the stated use of funds and supported by deposit volume, rather than to a maximum.
Receivables purchased
Specified dollar amount of future receivables set at closing.
Remittance
Daily or weekly ACH against the specified percentage of receivables.
Tranching
Larger needs may be structured in stages, with a follow-on purchase after performance on the first.
Duration
No fixed term. Set by the pace at which receivables are collected.

Required documents

  • Completed application, including the intended use of funds
  • Last 3–4 months of complete business bank statements
  • Photo ID for the signing owner and voided business check
  • Supporting documentation for the use of funds where relevant — supplier quote, purchase order, signed contract, or invoice
  • Current lease or proof of premises, if requested

Solution 03

Renewals & refinancing

Existing ACA Funding Group merchants who have delivered a meaningful portion of the receivables purchased can renew for additional capital. A new receivables purchase agreement is entered into, the remaining balance of the prior agreement is netted out of the new purchase price, and the business receives the difference.

Who it fits: merchants with a clean remittance history — consistent ACH delivery, no unauthorized account changes, and stable or improving deposit volume. Performance on the prior position is the primary consideration.

Typical structure

Eligibility
Generally considered once a substantial share of the specified amount has been remitted; the exact threshold is set by underwriting.
Net funding
New purchase price less the outstanding balance on the existing agreement, so only one position remains.
Remittance
Reset under the new agreement, daily or weekly by ACH.
Documents on file
Renewals typically require only updated statements, not a full re-application.

Required documents

  • Most recent 3 months of business bank statements
  • Confirmation of any positions taken on since the original funding
  • Updated ownership or banking details, if changed
  • Signed renewal receivables purchase agreement

Solution 04

Consolidation of stacked positions

When a business is carrying several advances simultaneously — commonly called stacking — the combined daily obligation can exceed what collections comfortably support. In a consolidation, ACA Funding Group purchases receivables in an amount sufficient to pay off multiple outstanding positions, leaving the business with a single agreement and a single remittance schedule.

Who it fits: businesses with two or more active positions, still generating consistent deposits, where reducing the aggregate daily obligation would restore operating cash flow. Payoffs are sent directly to the existing funders.

Typical structure

Payoff mechanics
Payoff letters are obtained from each existing funder; funds are disbursed directly to those funders at closing.
Purchase price
Covers the aggregate payoff amounts, with any remaining balance released to the business where underwriting supports it.
Remittance
One consolidated daily or weekly ACH, set against a specified percentage of receivables.
Conditions
Typically requires that consolidated positions be closed out and that no new positions be taken during the agreement.

Required documents

  • Completed application listing every open position
  • Last 4 months of complete business bank statements
  • Current payoff letter or balance statement from each existing funder
  • Copies of the existing agreements, if available
  • Photo ID for the signing owner and voided business check

Solution 05

Revenue-based funding for seasonal businesses

For businesses whose revenue is concentrated in part of the year, remittance is structured as a percentage of collected receivables rather than a flat fixed debit. When volume falls, the amount delivered falls with it; when the season peaks, delivery accelerates.

Who it fits: landscaping and snow removal, seasonal retail and tourism, HVAC, event and catering businesses, and any operation with a documented annual cycle visible across 12 months of statements.

Typical structure

Specified percentage
Remittance drawn as an agreed share of receivables, so delivery moves with actual collections.
Remittance frequency
Weekly is common for seasonal files; daily where deposit activity is continuous.
Reconciliation
Periodic reconciliation against bank and processing records where a fixed estimated debit is used.
Timing
Files are often funded ahead of the season, against the receivables the season is expected to generate.
Duration
No fixed term; delivery ends when the specified amount has been remitted in full.

Required documents

  • Completed application noting the business's seasonal cycle
  • Up to 12 months of business bank statements, so peaks and troughs are visible
  • Merchant processing statements, where card volume is material
  • Signed contracts, route lists, or booking schedules for the upcoming season, if available
  • Photo ID for the signing owner and voided business check

Before you apply

Have your statements ready

Nearly every underwriting question is answered by the bank statements. Sending complete statements — every page, including the summary pages — is the single biggest factor in how quickly a file moves.

  • 3–4 months of business bank statements are required for every file. Seasonal reviews may request up to 12 months.
  • All pages, unedited. PDF statements downloaded from your bank are preferred over screenshots.
  • The operating account — the account business deposits actually land in, and from which remittance would be drawn.
  • Disclose open positions. Existing advances appear in the statements regardless; disclosing them up front avoids a second review cycle.
